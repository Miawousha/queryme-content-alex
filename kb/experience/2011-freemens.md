## Context
Freemens grew directly out of research at G2Elab (Grenoble INP / CNRS), where it began life as the **ACCIUM** incubation project, and was incorporated in **October 2011**, based in Saint-Martin-d'Hères on the Grenoble campus. Alexandre co-founded the company with researcher **Jean-Christophe Crébier**, a CNRS research director and specialist in power-electronics silicon integration. The founding idea was to bring power electronics on silicon *inside* the battery — managing energy flow between cells to extend life and improve safety, rather than treating the battery as a passive pack.

## Role
Co-founder and CEO — *Président* in French. Led the company from a two-person research spin-out to a commercial product company and through its acquisition. The team grew to nine engineers, several recruited directly as PhDs from G2Elab. Technical scope ran the full range — from ASIC design and power electronics through embedded firmware to cloud-based monitoring.

## Mission
Design **modular and safe** battery-management systems that extend battery life — and build the first core of a cloud platform to absorb the telemetry streaming off the embedded BMS.

## What we built
- **FreeSafe** — a chemistry-agnostic, modular battery management system (BMS) product family for high-power applications, shipped in two flavours: **FreeSafe Extended (FS-XT)** for high-end packs and **FreeSafe LT** for lighter installations.
- **Fusion** — the embedded firmware running on the FreeSafe hardware, including the state-estimation and balancing logic.
- **XARM** — a custom integrated chip (ASIC) embedding a complete multi-leg inverter on a 20 V process technology: power electronics brought directly onto silicon. It was the proof of concept — and a necessary condition — for making active balancing economically viable, since discrete solutions would have been too costly and unreliable.
- **FreeWay** — the first cloud platform built to absorb telemetry from the embedded BMS, for battery asset management and fleet monitoring.
- **BatLab** — battery emulation software used for BMS development and benchmarking.

Freemens served demanding, high-power use cases: electric vehicles, drones, speedboats, and electric racing. By the time of the ION acquisition the company had shipped to more than 25 customers, including Airbus Safran, NTN-SNR, and CarWatt.

## Technology and innovation
Freemens built cutting-edge battery technology: advanced **active balancing**, a modular **software architecture for battery management**, and both **distributed and centralized** battery-management architectures. The team pioneered **power networking** — masterless, hot-swappable, parallelisable batteries — the conceptual seed of the battery power-networking and meshing work later advanced at ION and Maxwell. Embedded software and electronics were developed in-house, with hardware production run through a partnership with **MAATEL**, an electronics-manufacturing company near Grenoble specialising in medical devices and led by CEO **Franck Degiovanni**, which managed the EMS subcontractors producing the boards in the Czech Republic and Eastern Europe.

## Research
Led the **OPHELI** research programme in collaboration with G2Elab, studying battery aging mechanisms in relation to real in-situ application usage. The work connected laboratory aging science to how batteries actually behave in the field, which fed directly into the state-estimation and life-prediction logic in the products. Alexandre also supervised a PhD thesis and hired several PhDs directly from G2Elab, building a research-grade engineering team.

## Funding and recognition
- Early institutional backing: **INP Entreprise** — represented by **Henri-Marc Michaud** — in 2012, followed by **Pertinence Invest** in 2013 and **Maatel** in 2015 — also Freemens' electronics manufacturing partner, based near Grenoble.
- **Grand Prix of EDF's *Concours Énergie Intelligente*** — first edition, awarded November 2012 in Lyon. Freemens was selected from 55 European projects by a jury chaired by Bruno Bonnell.
- **H2020 iModBatt** — €250 000 grant won in 2017, joining a 14-organisation European consortium designing a high-energy-density modular battery pack for automotive and stationary use. Freemens contributed a custom version of its BMS.

## Outcome
Freemens was acquired by **ION Energy** (Mumbai) in a cross-border cash-and-equity transaction announced **5 February 2018**. The full Freemens engineering and sales team and the FreeSafe / Fusion / FreeWay technology moved to ION. Alexandre joined ION as **co-founder and CTO**, and the team kept operating from the Grenoble area. Reporting at the time described it as a first-of-its-kind cross-border acquisition in the battery-management space — Akhil Aryan, ION's CEO, publicly credited Freemens' "unparalleled battery management know-how" as the reason for the deal.
