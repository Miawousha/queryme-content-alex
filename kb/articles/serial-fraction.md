Article by Alexandre on the Matrice blog. Its claim: monolith or modules was never really a question about code, but about the economics of building in parallel. It is a crash course in six ideas that settle the question (stable intermediate forms, modules that hide a decision, Conway's law, the coordination price of boundaries, modularity as an option, and sizing modules to the people who hold them), looks at where coordination actually lives, and re-runs the ledger for the case where the builders are agents.
